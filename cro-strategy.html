Part 1, up close

PACE Framework: Four Aspects of the Customer Journey

P

Prognosis

What is the prognosis a customer imagines? Will they solve a problem or pain-point? Are they attracted by the promise a product offers? What do they see happening after they buy it?

A

Activities

What actions do they take once they have decided to purchase an item? Are they researching benefits, comparing price, looking for reviews, or just slowly browsing until something catches their eye.

C

Criteria

What are the criteria which qualifies or disqualifies your product in their minds? Are they finding that info on your site or off of your site. Is it clear and getting the point across?

E

Event

Is there an event that drives conversions, whether it is a landing page, email, or a sale? Does the triggering event get interrupted anywhere in the conversion funnel? What opportunities are there to nudge more events.

The PACE framework gives us the perspective and psychology of the customer before we dive into the data and view their on-page behavior.

Data Analytics

The one I built

There is nothing worse than too much data. Most analytics drown a store in numbers that never point anywhere, so Snapshot captures the behavioural data that actually bears on conversion and leaves the rest alone.

A site snapshot shows the conversion rate and revenue coming from each page’s visitors, so we can tell which page is worth the hour before we spend it. A page snapshot then shows where those visitors came from and what they do once they land.

On the call this is usually what settles the argument: it turns “this page feels wrong” into a specific thing that happens to real shoppers, at a specific point on the page.
